# Copywriting

The words on a poster or menu do as much as the design. Generic AI copy ("Indulge in our delicious, handcrafted delights!") is the fastest way to make a nice design look like slop.

## Principles

1. **Facts first.** What is it, when, where, how much. Most posters only need those four things, plus one reason to care.
2. **Sound like the owner talking to a regular.** Ask the user: "How would you say this to a regular at the counter?" Use their words.
3. **Be specific.** Specific nouns and numbers beat adjectives. "48-hour dough" beats "artisanal". "Back Friday" beats "exciting news".
4. **Short.** Poster headline: 2–6 words. Supporting line: under 12 words. Menu description: under 12 words.
5. **One joke at most**, and only if it suits the owner's voice.
6. **Never invent facts:** no made-up awards, reviews, quotes, "best in town", "since 1985", prices, ingredients or sourcing claims. Use [PLACEHOLDERS] and flag them.

## Headline patterns that work

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| The plain fact | "Pho is back. Fridays." |
| The product, named | "Cardamom Buns" |
| The specific detail | "48 hours in the dough" |
| The time | "Open till 2am Fri & Sat" |
| The local reference | "Two doors down from the post office" |
| The instruction | "Bring your own cup. Get 50¢ off." |
| The dry aside | "Yes, we have oat milk." |
| The number | "3 tacos. $6." |

## Banned words and phrases

Don't use these unless the owner insists:

- **Verbs:** elevate, indulge, experience, discover, unleash, unlock, embark, savor (as a command), immerse, transform, ignite, delight
- **Adjectives:** delectable, decadent, mouthwatering, scrumptious, succulent, sumptuous, exquisite, tantalizing, irresistible, divine, heavenly, luscious, perfect/perfectly, ultimate, unforgettable, unparalleled, world-class, next-level, curated, bespoke, handcrafted, artisanal, elevated, premium, gourmet, authentic (unless it's a factual claim the owner made)
- **Phrases:** "a symphony of flavors", "a culinary journey", "where X meets Y", "crafted with love/passion/care", "taste the difference", "your new favorite", "a feast for the senses", "something for everyone", "look no further", "treat yourself", "you deserve it", "made with the finest ingredients", "a hidden gem", "nestled in the heart of", "tantalize your taste buds", "the perfect blend of", "join us for an unforgettable", "don't miss out"
- **Punctuation:** stacked exclamation marks, more than one exclamation mark per piece, emojis, hashtags on printed pieces.

## Menu descriptions

- List what's in it, in plain words, in the order you'd taste it: "Pork shoulder, salsa verde, pickled onion, soft roll."
- No adjective before every noun. One adjective per dish at most, and only if it carries information ("charred", "raw", "smoked").
- Use proper names only when they're real: the bakery that makes the bread, the farm, the cheese. Ask the user; don't invent sources.
- Dish names can carry personality ("The Dock St"), but the description must say what it is.
- Prices: no currency symbol on sit-down menus ("14", not "$14.00"), no ".00", no dot leaders. Counter menus and sale posters may use symbols where clarity matters.
- Dietary marks: one consistent system (V, VG, GF) with a legend at the bottom.

### Before → after

| Before (slop) | After |
|---|---|
| "Indulge in our decadent, velvety chocolate cake, lovingly crafted with the finest cocoa for an unforgettable treat!" | "Chocolate cake. Dark, dense, a bit salty. Cream on the side." |
| "Experience our signature burger: a mouthwatering symphony of juicy beef and premium toppings." | "Double smash patty, American cheese, onions, pickles, house sauce." |
| "Elevate your morning with our artisanal handcrafted coffee!" | "Coffee from 7am. Beans roasted by [ROASTER] in [CITY]." |
| "Join us for an unforgettable evening of live music and delicious bites!" | "Live jazz, Thursday 8pm. Kitchen open till 11." |
| "Grand Opening! Don't miss out on amazing deals!!!" | "We're open. First coffee's on us, Sat 3 May." |

## Event and sale posters: what must be on it

1. What: the thing, named plainly.
2. When: day and date and time. Write the date out ("Sat 14 June") so nobody misreads it.
3. Where: if it's not obvious (the poster is not in their window), include the address or a short landmark.
4. How much / how to: price, "free", "no booking", "book at [URL]".
5. Optional: one line of personality.

Check it against the brief. If any of these are missing, ask once or use a clearly marked placeholder.
